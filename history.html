Legion History

From a single room in Dublin to a worldwide lay apostolate

The Founding Night — September 7, 1921

On that fateful Wednesday night, September 7, 1921—the Vigil of the Nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary—at 8:00 p.m., fifteen women met Brother Frank Duff and Father Michael Toher in the usual meeting room at Myra House on Francis Street in Dublin, Ireland. What was their surprise to see that she whose name they were to bear was there before them!

They came to the meeting ready to serve as soldiers under the banner and patronage of Mary, and, as in the case of all proper armies, the commander was there, ready to receive their enrollments. When they entered the room, the table around which they were to meet—usually bare—was decked out just as for a present-day Praesidium meeting. There was a white cloth, a statue of the Immaculate Conception, two vases with flowers, and two candlesticks with lighted candles. The Queen was waiting for her soldiers. It was the happy thought of one of the earlier-comers, though no instructions had been given.

Initially known as the Association of Our Lady of Mercy, the young organization adopted the name Legion of Mary in November 1925 and modeled its structure and Latin terminology after the ancient Roman legion.

Early Expansion Across Five Continents (1921–1933)

From 1921 to 1926, the Legion's thirteen branches remained confined within the city walls of Dublin, where members began visiting the sick in hospitals and aiding the destitute. It was in 1927 that the young army began its march outward, with Waterford city welcoming its first branch outside Dublin.

From then on, expansion became rapid. Glasgow was reached in April 1928, and London followed in 1929. In 1931, the Legion reached Asia with a start in Madras (now Chennai), India. That same year saw its first foundation in North America at Raton, a large mining camp in New Mexico, where the first American legionaries were men.

In the autumn of 1932, the Legion crossed the Canadian border when a group of Cowichan men established the first Canadian Praesidium at Duncan, British Columbia. In 1932, the Legion also arrived in Australia. In 1933, foundations were established in South Africa—first in the Transvaal and shortly after at the Cape—and in September of that eventful year, the Legion was established in the West Indies.

Global Growth & Legacy Today

Having reached all five continents by 1933, the Legion continued its ceaseless global growth, carried forward by dedicated envoys such as Venerable Edel Quinn in East Africa during the 1930s and 1940s and Servant of God Alfie Lambe in South America during the 1950s. Praised by successive popes since Pope Pius XI and endorsed by the Second Vatican Council, the Legion was formally recognized by the Holy See as an International Association of the Faithful. Founder Frank Duff (1889–1980) saw his cause for beatification officially opened in 1996.

The Legion Today: What began around a single table in Dublin has grown into the largest apostolic organization of lay people in the Catholic Church, with over 3 million active members—and more than 10 million active and auxiliary (praying) members combined—serving in over 170 countries worldwide. The official Legion Handbook and Tessera prayers have been translated into more than 50 languages.
