Catholic Tradition & Devotion

Our Lady of Fatima

The story of Our Lady of Fatima is one of the most beloved in Catholic tradition. The Feast of Our Lady of Fatima is celebrated annually on May 13th to commemorate the first Marian apparition to three shepherd children in Fátima, Portugal, in 1917.

Key Details

  • Date: May 13th (marking the first apparition; minor regional or local observances also note October 13th, the date of the final apparition and the Miracle of the Sun).
  • History: Between May 13 and October 13, 1917, the Blessed Virgin Mary appeared six times to Lúcia dos Santos (age 9) and her cousins Francisco (age 8) and Jacinta Marto (age 6) at Cova da Iria.
  • Core Message: A call to daily recitation of the Holy Rosary, prayer, repentance, penance, and consecration for world peace.
  • The Final Apparition: On October 13, 1917, approximately 70,000 people witnessed the predicted Miracle of the Sun, where the sun appeared to dance, spin, and plunge toward the earth before returning to the sky.
  • Recognition: The local bishop approved the apparitions in 1930, and the feast day was added to the worldwide Catholic calendar in 2002. Millions of pilgrims visit the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Fátima each year.

Core Themes of Her Message

Our Lady of Fatima represents a profound call to faith, repentance, and devotion. Her messages revolve around key themes that remain deeply relevant:

Prayer

The Rosary is central to her message. She asked the children to pray it daily for peace and to end the suffering in the world.

Repentance

Mary calls for a sincere return to God, urging individuals and communities to turn away from sin and seek reconciliation.

Conversion

She emphasizes the importance of personal and societal transformation to align with God's will.

Sacrifice

The children were encouraged to make sacrifices for the reparation of sins and for the conversion of sinners.

Her message is one of timeless hope, urging humanity to work towards a future rooted in faith, justice, and mercy.
